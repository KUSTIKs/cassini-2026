# The three challenges

On TAIKAI these are called "Categories". The text below is from cassini.eu/hackathons, lightly condensed.
**Every challenge asks for products, devices or services that use European space data, information and signals from Copernicus and Galileo.**

## Challenge #1: Cybersecurity for space-based strategic enablers
**Context:** Galileo and Copernicus are vital for positioning, navigation, surveillance and monitoring. The more people depend on them, the more they become targets for cyberattacks, especially in conflict zones, where disruption can put people in danger. The cyber-resilience of critical services and infrastructure needs strengthening.

**Asks for solutions that:**
- **Validate signals** (for example, detect GNSS spoofing or jamming, or authenticate with Galileo OSNMA)
- **Identify anomalies using Earth observation data**
- Help **improve cybersecurity across Europe**

## Challenge #2: Space-enabled crisis response and civil resilience
**Context:** war and instability threaten safety, infrastructure and basic services. Humanitarian groups, first responders and civil authorities need real-time, trusted information to act.

**Asks for solutions that:**
- **Map conflict damage using Earth observation data**
- **Support routes for goods and people using Galileo**
- **Enhance the safety and resilience of civilian infrastructure**

**Target users named:** humanitarian actors, first responders, civil authorities.

## Challenge #3: Countering transnational crime and terrorism with space data
**Context:** conflicts weaken state control and create room for arms trafficking, smuggling and extremist networks. The EU needs tools to anticipate, track and respond to these threats, ideally before they reach its borders.

**Asks for solutions that:**
- **Monitor borders and critical infrastructure**
- **Detect smuggling routes and patterns**
- Strengthen the **safety and resilience of European nations**

## How to read them
- The theme is **peace, resilience and security**. Purely environmental or climate ideas need a clear security, crisis or crime angle to score well on relevance.
- Each challenge names concrete example directions. Mapping your idea onto one of them, in their own words, is the easiest way to score high on relevance.
- Use **both** Copernicus and Galileo where it makes sense. The challenge text names both, and the rules encourage combining EU space components.
