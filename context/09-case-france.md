# Case study: France's registers of vulnerable people

Researched 28 Nov 2026 from official French sources (links at the bottom). Anything no source covered is marked **not found**. Nothing here is from general knowledge.

France has **two separate schemes that never meet**:
1. **PHRV** (patients à haut risque vital): patients whose life depends on electricity. Run by the regional health agency (ARS) and the grid operators. Covers power cuts only.
2. **Town-hall register** (registre nominatif communal): older and disabled people living at home. Run by the mayor and the prefect. Covers heatwaves, extreme cold and health crises.

---

## 1. PHRV: patients whose life depends on electricity

**Legal basis:** circulars DGS/SQ2 n° 97-133 (17 Feb 1997) and DGS/DUS n° 2009-217 (16 Jul 2009).
**Who qualifies:** patients on a ventilator who can last **4 hours a day or less** without it (on it more than 20 hours a day), and children fed by IV at home (home parenteral nutrition).
**How many:** about **4,000** in all of France, with "a few hundred" more who never registered because the procedure is too complicated (Assemblée nationale written question 680, 2024).

### Parties
| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| **Patient** (or legal representative) | **Must start the process**, keep their details up to date and renew every year |
| **Family and friends** | Can phone the dedicated number to ask how long a cut will last; no formal role |
| **Family doctor** | Fills in the medical certificate |
| **ARS** (regional health agency), via its regional office | Reviews the file, approves it, sends the list to the grid operators. Also responsible for adding, renewing and removing patients |
| **Grid operators** (Enedis, local distributors; EDF in La Réunion) | Check the patient's address on the network, confirm to the patient, send warnings. Their duty is **"to inform, not to keep the power on"** |
| **Oxygen supplier** | 24/7 on-call service for patients on oxygen; spare bottle on request |
| **Hospital** | The patient has to know how to get admitted in an emergency |
| **Health ministry** | After Storm Ciarán, sent guidance to the ARS on home hospitalisation and dialysis patients during power-rationing |

### Flow
```
Registration:  patient fills in the request form + doctor's certificate (+ a patient
               questionnaire in Île-de-France) → posted to the regional ARS office
               → ARS approves → informs Enedis or the local distributor
               → grid operator checks the address on the network → confirms to the patient
               → valid 1 year → renew the same way; moving house (even temporarily)
                 = start again
Planned cut:   individual letter 5 days before
Power-rationing (Ecowatt red):  rolling 2-hour cuts at peak times; phone call 3 days before,
               2 days before, the day before. The patient is NOT exempt from cuts and
               needs their own backup power for more than 2 h
Unexpected cut / storm:  no warning is possible; the patient calls a dedicated number
               to find out how long it will last
```

### Data, checks and priority
- **Data:** request form, doctor's certificate, address, contact details (and a patient questionnaire in Île-de-France).
- **Checks:** the only check is the patient renewing every year. The grid operator phones only before power-rationing cuts. No source shows anyone calling patients to check on them during a storm.
- **Priority:** everyone on the list is top priority by definition (they can't last more than 4 h without their machine), but there's no ranking inside the list. Being on it gets you **advance warning**, not priority restoration of power. Age plays no part.

### Known failure
- After **Storm Ciarán**, a patient on a ventilator went **5 days without power** because she wasn't on Enedis's list, so the automatic emergency process never ran for her (written question 680).
- The ministry's reply (11 Mar 2025) explained the circulars and recommended that patients sign up to **Ecowatt alerts**, which are national, not personal. It proposed no simpler procedure.

### Not found
- The format and frequency of the list the ARS sends to grid operators.
- What Enedis does during a storm (calls, visits, generators), and the role of the prefect and the emergency medical service (SAMU).
- Any link to floods or fires: nothing in the scheme covers hazards other than power cuts.

## 2. Town-hall register of vulnerable people

**Legal basis:** art. L121-6-1 and R121-2 to R121-12 of the French social-action code (Code de l'action sociale et des familles). Recently strengthened by **Loi n° 2024-317 of 8 April 2024** (art. 8) and **Décret n° 2026-590 of 3 July 2026**.
**Who can sign up:** people aged 65+, people 60+ officially unable to work, and disabled adults receiving certain benefits or an invalidity pension, **living at home**. Second homes count. Care-home residents are excluded because they're covered by a separate scheme ("plans bleus").
**Data recorded:** name, date of birth, which category they're in, address, phone, home-care service and/or emergency contact, date of the request, and who made it if it was a third party. Mayors may not collect anything beyond this.

### Parties
| Party | Role |
|---|---|
| **Mayor** | Must set up and keep the register; a power that belongs to the mayor personally, not the town council. Four duties: inform residents, collect requests, keep the register up to date and confidential, hand it to the **prefect only**, on request |
| **Staff the mayor names** | The only people allowed access; bound by professional secrecy |
| **The person, or their legal representative** | Asks to be registered; can see, correct and remove their entry at any time |
| **Third parties** (family, neighbour, family doctor, social-action office (CCAS), home-care service) | Can ask for someone to be registered, **in writing, one person at a time**; swapping lists of names is not allowed |
| **Prefect** (state's representative in the département) | Triggers the alert and emergency plan, requests the register, and alone decides who else gets it |
| **Home-care services and agencies** | Receive all or part of the data on the prefect's instruction and contact people |
| **CNIL** (data protection authority) | Any use beyond the set purpose needs a declaration to it first |
| **Département + pension fund** (since the 2024 law) | Send in data on people receiving disability or old-age care benefits (APA, PCH, pension-fund support), unless the person objects |

### Flow
```
Registration:  request by any channel the mayor provides (form, phone, email); third
               parties must write → mayor sends acknowledgement within 8 days, which
               also explains how to be removed (if the person doesn't object, they're
               registered)
Alert (heatwave, extreme cold, health crisis…):
               prefect triggers the plan → asks the mayor for the register
               → sent by registered letter, by courier or to the prefect's secure mailbox
               → prefect lets home-care services and agencies use it → contact
               (whether by phone or visit: not found)
Corrections:   services that find wrong data tell the mayor, who updates it and informs
               the person
Removal:       on request, if the person moves away, or if they die
```

### What real town forms ask (Nancy, Frontignan, Creutzwald, Ormes)
| Town | Fields beyond the legal minimum | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nancy** (third-party form) | Type of home service (home help, home nursing, other, none) with its address and phone | Third party ticks their role: legal representative, home-care service, family doctor, other. Sent "sous pli confidentiel" to the town's social-action office (CCAS). Purpose includes "organising a periodic contact" during an alert |
| **Frontignan** | Landline, mobile, **email**; relationship of the emergency contact | A third party must be "specially authorised in writing" by the person. Covers "heatwaves, extreme cold, epidemics…" |
| **Creutzwald** | **Lives alone / as a couple / no family nearby**; extra category "**isolated person**" | Signatory accepts the duty to report any change to the town |
| **Ormes** (data-protection notice only) | — | Legal basis: GDPR art. 6.1.e (public-interest task). Data "updated each year" |

- **No form asks about health, equipment, mobility, floor or lift.** The law limits what can be collected.
- **Periods away:** no form asks for them. The only duty is to report a change of address (art. R121-5); moving out of the town = removed from the register (Frontignan).
- **During an alert:** the stated purpose is a "periodic contact" (Nancy). How often, by whom, and what happens if the person doesn't answer: **not found**.
- **Retention:** until the person dies or asks to be removed (Nancy and Creutzwald, citing art. R121-11).

### Who is prioritised?
- Being 65+ (or 60+ and unable to work, or disabled) only makes someone **eligible** to sign up. Nobody is added because of age alone, except benefit recipients fed in under the 2024 law.
- During an alert, everyone on the register is to be contacted. **No source describes an order** (e.g. "lives alone first" or "oldest first").
- The data allowed is too thin to rank anyway: no health need, no mobility, no floor. Only Creutzwald asks whether the person lives alone.

### Where the data comes from
- Until 2024, mayors were **not allowed** to get names from social-security bodies (Conseil d'État opinion of 20 July 2004). They could use the electoral roll (name and address) to contact residents (DGS FAQ).
- Since the 2024 law, the **département and the pension fund** send in benefit recipients, unless the person objects. **The technical channel and format: not found** (it would be in Décret 2026-590, not read).

### What changed in 2024 and 2026
- **Before:** the register's only purpose was the alert and emergency plan. Mayors were **not allowed** to ask social-security bodies for names (Conseil d'État opinion of 20 July 2004).
- **After the 2024 law and 2026 decree:** data on benefit recipients is fed in automatically, with the right to object. The purpose widens to **ongoing contact, fighting isolation and helping people claim benefits they're entitled to** (Landot & Associés, July 2026).

### Limitations
- Only **1–5 % of eligible people are registered** (Landot & Associés).
- No requirement to cover everyone ("aucun impératif d'exhaustivité").
- It's deliberately offline: the health ministry FAQ says it must **never be openly accessible or on a network**.
- **No map.** Floods aren't among the listed emergencies (the list reads heatwave, extreme cold, health crisis, followed by "…").
- Towns will need new resources to run the reformed register (Landot & Associés).

### Not found
- The opposition period for the automatic data feeds (a search result mentioned 2 months, unconfirmed).
- How long data is kept under the 2026 decree.

## What it means for FirstReach
- **The lists exist but are fragmented:** PHRV goes ARS → grid operator; the town register goes mayor → prefect. Neither is linked to the other or to a hazard map.
- **Both cover the wrong hazards for a flood:** PHRV is about power cuts only, the town register about heat, cold and health crises. A fast flood falls between them.
- **Both are slow and on paper:** posted forms, renewal every year, registered letters to the prefect.
- **Neither system ranks people or knows whether they're home right now.** PHRV has the medical need but no age or living situation; the town register has age, disability and sometimes "lives alone" but no medical need. FirstReach's priority rule combines both, and a status check (home or away, battery or oxygen time) at the start of an event would fill a real gap.
- **Built-in privacy rules to respect:** one purpose only, the prefect decides who receives data, staff named by the mayor, no open network access. FirstReach's design should mirror these (roles, audit log, sharing only once an emergency is declared).
